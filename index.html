OVERVIEW

The state of Leonida

wish you were here

Six regions, one long peninsula and a lot of coastline: the neon of Vice City in the south-east, the Keys trailing off the tip, the Grassrivers wetlands in the middle, and Port Gellhorn, Ambrosia and Mount Kalaga to the north and west.

REGION

The Leonida Keys

island time

Wish you were here: the Keys, postcard-ready.

Strung off the southern tip of Leonida like a broken necklace, the Keys are where the state slows down, sobers up a little, and goes fishing. One road, a lot of water, and everybody knows everybody's business.

Getting there

Drive south out of Vice-Dale County and pick up the Keys Causeway, which lands you on Key Lento. From there it's Route 1 all the way down: long, low bridges with the Gulf on one side and the Atlantic glare on the other. Go at sunset. Go slowly. If you're in a hurry, you're in the wrong region.

The islands

Key Lento is the main town and the closest thing the Keys have to a high street: a marina, a bait-and-beer bar called the Rusty Anchor, and Kenton's Island Dreams dive shop, the place to start if you fancy treasure under the waves. Further down the chain, Catalan Key has the lighthouse, and East Key and Goose Key are quieter still.

Don't miss

A wreck dive with Kenton. Pick up a metal detector, follow the pink search rings and bring something shiny back to the surface. It's the Keys' oldest pastime, with more paperwork.

The real Florida

Key Lento is a nod to Key Largo, the largest island in the real Florida Keys. Route 1 echoes the Overseas Highway (US 1), and keep an eye out for a very familiar-looking long bridge, the game's take on the Seven Mile Bridge.

Best for diving, sunsets, boats and keeping a low profile.

REGION • VICE-DALE COUNTY

Vice City

sun and fun

Ocean Beach, as the postcards promise it.

Leonida's neon capital sells itself as America's sun and fun capital, and for once the brochure isn't lying. It's loud, beautiful, expensive and slightly out of control, often all on the same block.

Getting there

Vice City is the county seat of Vice-Dale County, filling the south-east corner of the state. The beach districts sit on a barrier island across the causeways from the mainland, and the city's metro is the easiest way to hop between the two once the traffic sets in.

Neighbourhoods

Ocean Beach is the postcard: pastel Art Deco hotels, white sand and a tourist strip that never really sleeps. Just south, South Beach is where the neon and the nightclubs take over. Back on the mainland, Downtown is all glass towers and money, while Little Cuba trades in strong coffee and panaderías, and La Perle is the grittier, Caribbean-flavoured neighbourhood the tourists rarely see. Down on the water, the VC Port keeps the city stocked, legitimately and otherwise.

Don't miss

The Tisha-Wocka flea market. Rockstar name-checks it as a Vice City essential, and it's the place to find something you didn't know you needed and probably shouldn't own.

The real Florida

Vice City is Miami. Ocean Beach is South Beach and its Art Deco Historic District along Ocean Drive, Little Cuba is Little Havana and Calle Ocho, Downtown's towers echo Brickell, and La Perle is Little Haiti.

Best for nightlife, beaches, people-watching and spending money you don't have.

REGION • THE SOUTHERN WETLANDS

Grassrivers

there be gators

Golden hour on the river of grass.

South of everything and wetter than anywhere, Grassrivers is Leonida with the pavement taken away: sawgrass to the horizon, mangroves at the edges, and something with teeth just under the surface.

Getting there

The wetlands spread across the south of the state, just above the Keys and south-west of Vice City. Most cars are hopeless out here, so it's an airboat or nothing. Expect a lot of noise and a lot of spray.

What's there

Mostly water, grass and wildlife: more than 170 species, from alligators and flamingos to pelicans and manatees. Hunters work the edges of the marsh, and the state's POACH wildlife officers patrol it to keep them honest. The odd shack, bait shop and boat ramp is about as close as Grassrivers gets to a town.

Don't miss

An airboat run at sunset. Cut the engine in the middle of nowhere, wait, and let the flamingos and gators come to you. Bring the camera: it's the best place in the state to fill up the Wildlife tab.

The real Florida

Grassrivers is the Everglades, the vast, slow-moving "river of grass" that drains south from Lake Okeechobee to Florida Bay. Airboat tours are a real Florida rite of passage.

Best for wildlife, airboats, photography and getting away from absolutely everyone.

REGION • KELLY COUNTY

Port Gellhorn

the forgotten coast

A beach town that's seen better decades.

Rockstar calls it a failed resort on a forgotten coast, and Port Gellhorn wears that description like a faded souvenir T-shirt. The sea's still nice. Almost nothing else is new.

Getting there

Port Gellhorn sits on the Gulf side of the state in Kelly County, north-west of Vice City. The coastal freeway runs the length of the region, and the town spills off it into motels, strip malls and trailer parks.

What's there

The Starlet Motel (ask Linda about the rent), the Delights strip club, the Crossroad Park Minimall, Moses' garage and a lot of places that buy and sell things without asking questions. Out of town, Kelly County has the real entertainment: the Gellhorn International Raceway and, off Route 32, the Thrillbilly Mud Club.

Don't miss

A night at the races. The Gellhorn International Raceway is proper circuit racing with laps and a timer, and the cleanest fun in town.

The real Florida

Port Gellhorn is modelled on Panama City and the surrounding Bay County towns on the Panhandle's Gulf coast: once a booming spring-break resort, now a lot quieter.

Best for cheap rooms, racing, mud and laying low.

REGION • AMBROSIA COUNTY

Ambrosia

keeping Leonida sweet

Cane fields, smokestacks and the refinery that runs the town.

In the middle of the state, on the shore of Lake Leonida, Ambrosia runs on sugar, steel and old-school values. The refinery gives the town its jobs; the bikers, by Rockstar's account, provide almost everything else.

Getting there

Ambrosia is Leonida's inland heart, on the south-western bank of Lake Leonida. Follow the highway signs out of Vice City and keep going until the skyline turns into smokestacks and the fields start smouldering.

What's there

The Allied Crystal sugar refinery dominates everything: the skyline, the jobs and, word has it, the local politics. Around it are miles of cane field, freight yards and the kind of small-town main street where everybody knows whose bike is outside the bar. That bike probably belongs to the Final Chapter MC.

Don't miss

The refinery after dark. Allied Crystal lit up at night, with smoke drifting over the cane, is one of the most striking sights in the state. Admire it from the road.

The real Florida

Ambrosia is Clewiston, "America's Sweetest Town", on the south shore of Lake Okeechobee, where sugar has been the main industry for generations and cane is still burned before harvest.

Best for road trips, bikers, small-town bars and big skies.

REGION • THE NORTHERN BORDER

Mount Kalaga

wild, wild country

Pine forest and canyon country on the state line.

Up on the northern border with Gloriana, Leonida finally gets some altitude. Mount Kalaga is the state's great outdoors: forest, rivers, canyons, and a backwoods population Rockstar describes as hillbilly mystics and paranoid radicals.

Getting there

Head north until the swamp turns into pine forest and the roads start to climb. The national park runs along the Gloriana state line, with winding roads, bridges and plenty of dirt tracks for anything with off-road tyres.

What's there

Rockstar sells it on three things: hunting, fishing and off-road trails. Add kayaking through the canyons, dirt bikes and quads tearing up the mud, and deer and big cats in the woods. Out here, the locals would rather you kept driving.

Don't miss

A hunting trip. Mount Kalaga is the home of Leonida's hunting activity: bring a rifle, pick a ridge, and try not to end up as a story the locals tell.

The real Florida

Real Florida doesn't really do mountains: its highest natural point, Britton Hill near the Alabama line, is only 345 feet. Mount Kalaga borrows the forested hills of the Florida–Georgia line and turns them up a notch.

Best for hunting, fishing, dirt bikes, kayaks and nobody asking where you've been.

Every road trip has one: the motel manager who has heard every story, believed none of them and would like the money now, please.

Linda runs the Starlet Motel the way a lighthouse runs a coastline: she isn't there to make you feel welcome, she's there so you know where the rocks are. When Lucia and Jason turn up needing somewhere to lie low, the conversation is short and almost entirely about money. A night costs what a night costs, and it's due before the key leaves the counter.

She's back again when the rent slips. Linda doesn't do threats, exactly. She does reminders, delivered with the flat patience of someone who has evicted tougher guests than you.

Why she matters

Leonida is a state of grand schemes, and Linda is its counterweight: the small, unglamorous reality of a bill that has to be paid. For Lucia and Jason, even standing still costs money.

Local etiquette: pay up front, don't haggle, and don't ask about the ice machine.

Out past the edges of the map that tourists bother with, Leonida keeps its weekend religion: engines, beer and a bowl of mud deep enough to lose a truck in. The Thrillbilly Mud Club is where the state's off-road crowd comes to race, show off a lift kit and get gloriously, irreversibly filthy.

Wyman tips you off about Billy's dirt races, and the club appears on your map afterwards. Stop by the merch stand at the entrance first: the Thrillbilly shirts and vests are the dress code, and both Jason and Lucia can kit out.

Our tip

If you care about the story more than the podium, just finish clean. If you want the full $2,500, read the corners early and don't try to barge through every rider.

The real Florida

The club is a nod to Redneck Mud Park near Punta Gorda, an 880-acre stretch of mud bowls and trails outside Fort Myers. It throws big spring and autumn weekends that draw crowds in the tens of thousands.

WILDLIFE • FIELD RECORD

Wildlife

don't feed the gators

Leonida is wilder than its postcards. Get out of the car, get the camera out, and the state starts filling up with things that bite, fly, swim and occasionally sunbathe on the golf course.

Studying an animal adds it to your database. For 100% you need 100 of the 255 animals, so you don't need to find every last heron.

By group

Group sizes come from an early guide, so treat them as rough. "Other" covers the animals they leave out, so everything adds up to 255.

The real Florida

The Everglades is one of the most invaded wild places in the US. Burmese pythons, released or escaped pets, are now established across the real wetlands, and Florida runs public hunts to try to keep them in check.

King Gator Golf

The big gator presides over the greens.

WHEREBuckskin Swamp, Vice-Dale County
HOLES18

If Leonida had a state sport, it might well be adventure golf. King Gator is roadside Florida at its most gloriously over the top: themed putting holes with ramps, obstacles and moving parts, all overseen by a reptile with no interest in your handicap. Fewest strokes wins, and the course is open any time, so there's no rush.

Our tip: treat each hole as a puzzle first and a putt second. Watch the moving parts go round once before you swing, and save the power shots for the ramps.

Flash FM is the city's oldest pop habit. Back in the Vice City era it ran on big-haired synthpop and new wave; today it's a glossy, sweat-on-the-dancefloor mix of current pop, club records and the kind of 2000s throwbacks you didn't realise you still knew every word of.
